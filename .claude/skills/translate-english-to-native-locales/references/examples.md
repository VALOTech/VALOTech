# Translation examples

## Ambiguous UI label
Source: `Upgrade`

Do not translate without knowing whether the action means moving to a paid plan, updating software, or changing an account tier.

## Technical term
Source: `Clean Room`

Do not automatically create a literal local-language term. Follow the approved glossary and established target-market usage. Retain English when it is the recognized term, with a short first-use explanation if useful.

## Obligation
Distinctions among `may`, `should`, `must`, `required`, and `prohibited` are protected meaning. Naturalness does not justify changing them.

## Qualification preservation
Source: Based on the evidence reviewed, the control appears to operate as designed, except for two systems that were not included in the sample.

A valid target must preserve all four elements: the conclusion is evidence-based, it is qualified by “appears”, the exception concerns two systems, and those systems were outside the sample.

## Impossible length constraint
Source CTA: Review and accept the updated data-processing terms.

If the field cannot fit both actions and the document allows only one CTA, do not silently remove “review” or “accept”. Flag the product decision and provide the shortest safe candidate.

## Structured string
Source: `<b>{name}</b> has %1$d pending reviews.`

The target may reorder the sentence, but must preserve the paired tags, `{name}`, `%1$d`, and the meaning of “pending”.
